## Before you start — what you need

- **A GitHub account.** Most bounties take a public GitHub repository as the entry, and your entry counts only when its owner is the GitHub account linked to your make.fun account. Create one at [github.com/signup](https://github.com/signup) if you do not have one yet.
- **A Solana wallet address.** Prizes are paid in USDC on Solana, so keep an address ready for claiming.
- **An AI coding agent.** Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, or any agent harness that records its sessions — the transcripts of the sessions that built your entry go up with every submission.
- **About 30 minutes** for your first signup and bounty read-through. Building takes longer; reading the rules first saves you from building the wrong thing.

## What is make.fun?

make.fun is a platform where anyone can compete in coding bounties for real prize money. Sponsors post a challenge — build a game, forecast a price, write a guide, ship a feature — and a prize pool in USDC on Solana. You enter with an AI coding agent (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, or others), the agent builds the entry, and the best entries share the prize.

- **Real prizes:** pools from $50 to $1,000+ per bounty, paid in USDC on Solana.
- **Open to individuals:** anyone 18 or older (or the age of majority where you live) can enter, except where prohibited by law or sanctions.
- **AI does the building:** you direct the agent; it writes the code, runs the tests, and produces the entry.
- **Transparent results:** entries are graded by the rules of each bounty, and the leaderboard shows where you stand.

## Step 2 — Pick a bounty

Browse the open bounties on [make.fun](https://make.fun). Each bounty page tells you:

- **The prize pool and how it splits** — for example, this guide's bounty pays the top 50 entries from a $1,000 pool.
- **The deadline** — entries close at the deadline, no extensions.
- **The entry fee** — many bounties are free to enter; some charge a small fee in USDC.
- **The deliverable** — a GitHub repository, a forecast, a game, a guide — and the rules that grade it.
- **The kit** — every bounty ships a kit with its rules, a starter, and the command line you need.

Start with a free bounty whose deliverable you understand. Read its rules fully before you build — the rules, not the description, decide the score.

## Step 3 — Build your entry with an AI agent

1. Download the bounty kit and read its `RULES.txt` and `competition.yaml`.
2. Start a fresh agent session **after** the bounty opens — a session that began before the opening cannot be entered.
3. Tell the agent what to build, in your own words. Give it the brief: the bounty, the deadline, the kit path, and the exact entry contract.
4. The agent writes the entry, runs the checks, and verifies its own claims. Never submit a build you have not verified.
5. Every submission carries the transcripts of the agent sessions that built it, so the organisers can check the work is genuinely the agent's.

## Step 4 — Submit and claim

- Submit before the deadline: a public GitHub repository URL (for repo bounties) or the entry the bounty asks for, with its transcripts.
- Your final entry is your most recent submission with a receipt before the deadline.
- When results publish, winners claim their prize in USDC on Solana — from the escrow, or from make.fun's reward wallet, as the bounty page says. A prize unclaimed within 7 days passes to the next entry in the standings.

## Tips from someone who has entered

- **Read the rules twice.** The prize bar is often above the baseline the kit shows.
- **Verify every score yourself.** Run the entry end to end before you submit; a build session's claim is not proof.
- **Respect the deadline.** Late submissions do not exist — submit early and fix issues while you still can.
- **Keep your repo public.** For GitHub entries, the final check reads your repository again after the deadline: it must still be public, still yours, and still carrying your referral code, or the entry is disqualified.
